\paragraph{\textbf{Can filtered training data improve summarization quality?}} Yes for this evaluation setting. Every aggregate measure in Table~\ref{tab:extension_metrics} increases under SIDE-filtered training. The gains span strict lexical overlap (BLEU-4: 5.4121 to 6.3185), sequence overlap (ROUGE-L: 33.4254 to 34.7324), and less exact reference comparisons (METEOR: 17.0302 to 18.5237; ChrF: 24.3055 to 27.4066). The largest absolute change is the 3.1011-point increase in ChrF. TF--IDF cosine also rises, though more modestly, from 17.1617 to 17.5747.

The pattern is not confined to reference-based metrics. The GPT-5.3 judge rises from 2.5080 to 3.1020 on the 1--5 scale, providing code-grounded evidence consistent with the automatic scores. This does not establish that every retained pair is superior; it shows that, under a size-matched training comparison, the filtered condition yields stronger average scores on the shared 500-example evaluation set.

The rank relationships move in the same direction. Spearman correlations between SIDE and the other metrics increase for all six measures. The most pronounced increase is for TF--IDF cosine (0.52 to 0.59), while ROUGE-L reaches the highest post-filtering correlation (0.60). These results are consistent with the intended role of filtering: it selects training pairs whose code-summary alignment produces outputs that are more consistently recognized by independent evaluation signals.

\paragraph{\textbf{How do the output diagnostics differ after training with different datasets?}} Table~\ref{tab:output_diagnostics} shows a more stable output distribution after filtering. The mean length rises from 7.934 to 9.398 tokens, the median from 7 to 8, and the upper quartile from 9 to 11. The first quartile remains six tokens, so short outputs are still present, but the failure mode that matters most in practice is substantially less frequent: empty summaries fall from 23 of 500 (4.60\%) to 6 of 500 (1.20\%).

At the other end of the distribution, the maximum length decreases from 42 to 34 tokens. Higher typical lengths together with fewer empty outputs and a lower maximum are consistent with less degenerate generation, rather than a simple shift toward verbosity. The diagnostics therefore qualify the aggregate metric gains: the filtered model improves measured quality while producing fewer unusable outputs.
